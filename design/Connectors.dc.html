Drawn

One hairline curve per child, drawn from between the parents to that child — so the horizontal bar disappears entirely, and with it the alignment problem. A line from each parent would be just as true and would cross itself on the way down, because the spouse sits to one side. Marriage stops being a line of the same kind and becomes a double rule, which is what genealogy has always used to mean it.

Why the current one looks wrong

The bar is drawn at fixed percentages — left:16%; right:16% — rather than to the children it joins. Measured in the running app it overshoots the left child by 39px and the right by 62px, and unevenly, because each child block is a different width once a spouse is attached. So it never quite lines up with the ticks below it, which is what the eye is objecting to before it notices the weight.

Curves also carry weight better than right angles: a 1.25px stroke would look thin and broken as an elbow, but reads as a continuous drawn line as a curve. Descent stays olive; marriage takes a warm neutral so the two are told apart by colour as well as by form.
