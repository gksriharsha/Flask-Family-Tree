Trunk

The conventional genealogical form, drawn correctly: a trunk from between the parents, a sibling bar spanning exactly the children it joins, and a short drop into each. The bar is measured rather than set at a percentage, so it cannot overshoot. A child linked by anything other than birth has a dashed drop.

Why the current one looks wrong

The bar is drawn at fixed percentages — left:16%; right:16% — rather than to the children it joins. Measured in the running app it overshoots the left child by 39px and the right by 62px, and unevenly, because each child block is a different width once a spouse is attached. So it never quite lines up with the ticks below it, which is what the eye is objecting to before it notices the weight.

Why not curves. An S-curve only reads well when the drop is comparable to the sideways run. Here the couple can sit 250px to the right of a child while only 150px above them, so the curve swoops sideways like a cable rather than descending like a lineage. Right angles are indifferent to that ratio, which is why every printed family chart uses them. Descent stays olive; marriage takes a warm neutral, so the two are told apart by colour as well as by form.
